E-Commerce Sales Analytics

Primeor Solutions internship project · Excel · MySQL · Power BI
Problem statement
Understand which categories, customer segments and markets drive sales and profit for an e-commerce business, using four years of transactions (2011–2014).
Dataset source
Primeor Solutions internship e-commerce sales dataset: 51,325 raw records and 21 columns covering orders, customers, products, sales, quantity, discount, profit and shipping, for 2011–2014. After cleaning, 51,181 records remained.
Project steps
  • Cleaned the raw data in Excel (51,325 to 51,181 records), then built pivot tables, formulas and charts for top products, loss-making products, sales by region, profit by category, monthly trend and segment-wise sales.
  • Wrote 10 MySQL queries on the sales data: most profitable products, top customers, sales by region and market, average profit by category, negative-profit orders, monthly sales trend, top sub-categories and ship mode usage.
  • Built a 3-page interactive Power BI dashboard (Executive Overview, Sales Analysis, Products & Customers) with KPI cards and Category, Region and Year slicers.
Key insights
  • $12.6M in sales and about $1.47M profit (~11.6% margin).
  • Technology led with 37.5% of sales.
  • Consumer was the largest segment (~51.5%) and APAC the top market (~28%).
  • Sales grew about 93% from 2011 to 2014, with monthly sales peaking in November and December.
  • Copiers were the most profitable sub-category ($258K), while Tables made a loss ($64K).
  • Standard Class was the most used ship mode ($7.56M of sales).
Dashboard / visualization
Business impact
Shows where sales and profit come from, and flags loss-making products and sub-categories such as Tables, so category, segment and market decisions can be based on data.

HR Analytics Dashboard

DataMites capstone project · Power BI · Excel · SQL
Problem statement
Analyse employee data to give the company a clear view of workforce size, remote vs headquarters split, average age, hiring rate over time and employee mix by gender, state, department and race, and suggest how to use these insights.
Dataset source
DataMites HR_Analytics employee dataset (22,214 records) from the DataMites project database, including age, gender, race, department, job title, location, hire date and termination date.
Project steps
  • Retrieved the employee data from the project database.
  • Built KPI cards for total employees, HQ vs remote %, and average age.
  • Created a hiring-trend line chart, a gender donut chart, and bar charts for state, department and race.
  • Added Department, Location, Gender and Year slicers, then wrote the analysis report and recommendations.
Key insights
  • 22,214 employees, average age 38.43 years.
  • 75.25% work at HQ and 24.75% remote.
  • Gender split: Male 50.81%, Female 46.46%, Non-Conforming 2.72%.
  • Engineering is the largest department (~6.7K), followed by Accounting (~3.3K).
  • Ohio is the largest state by a wide margin (~18.0K).
  • Hiring rose sharply early on, then stayed fairly steady with year-to-year changes.
Dashboard / visualization
Business impact
Workforce distribution can guide office-space planning, and hiring trends can guide recruitment planning. The report also recommends comparing hiring with attrition and adding measures such as tenure and time-to-hire.

Customer Segmentation Analytics

DataMites client project · MySQL · Excel · Orange
Problem statement
Using shopper purchase data from the U.S. market, decide which product categories should get discounts, understand card spending by age, season and location, and cluster customers into groups with targeted marketing campaigns.
Dataset source
DataMites Shopping Trends dataset: 3,900 customer records and 18 columns, including age, gender, category, purchase amount, location, season, discount and promo-code use, previous purchases and purchase frequency.
Project steps
  • Used MySQL to analyse purchases by category, discount status, age group, card type (credit vs debit), season, location and purchase frequency.
  • Applied K-Means clustering in Orange with three clusters and normalisation (average silhouette score 0.52, moderate separation).
  • Summarised the clusters in Excel and wrote the report, presentation and video walkthrough.
Key insights
  • Clothing had the highest total spend ($208,775) and Accessories the most purchases (820).
  • Accessories had the highest share of discounted purchases (50.7%).
  • Customers aged 46+ contributed the most total spend; the 36–45 group had the highest average purchase ($260).
  • Spring was the strongest season by purchases and total spend.
  • Spending amount, more than age, separated the three clusters: high (1,265 customers, avg $394), medium (1,406, avg $242) and low (1,229, avg $128).
Dashboard / visualization
Business impact
High-spending: loyalty rewards, early access and premium bundles. Medium-spending: cross-selling and seasonal offers. Low-spending: affordable bundles and value offers. Each campaign is meant to be tested against a control group and tracked on repeat purchases and profit.
